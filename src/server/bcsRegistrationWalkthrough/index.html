BCS Registration on NPWD

Phase 1 · steps 01–02

Before the system

The journey starts outside NPWD. A prospective scheme finds an information page, phones the Environment Agency, and the EA runs informal viability checks before anything is created on the system — the fee is substantial, so there is no point setting up an account for an applicant who cannot qualify.

01

The public entry point

Off-system02:07

New battery users land on the NPWD public welcome page — a news and guidance page, not a service start point. “New Batteries Users Start Here” in the sidebar is as close as NPWD gets to onboarding.

02

“Please call your appropriate authority”

Off-system02:18

The Batteries Home page tells would-be compliance schemes to phone their appropriate authority. The first real step — discussing whether the applicant has a viable operational plan and can meet its obligations — happens entirely off-system, against a working checklist the EA keeps.

Observation

The pre-application questions are asked again, formally, during the approval stage. Gareth will share the checklist spreadsheet.

Phase 2 · steps 03–09

Regulator sets up the account

Once the EA is satisfied the applicant could operate as a scheme, a regulator super user creates the organisation and its first user by hand. Everything here is done for the scheme, before the scheme has ever logged in.

03

Add a new compliance scheme

Regulator (EA)03:57

From Batteries Home → Compliance Schemes, the scheme list has a plain “To add a new compliance scheme click here” link. Adding a scheme needs regulator super user access. The demo scheme “MPG” is invented test data on the stage system.

04

The organisation record

Regulator (EA)03:40

The new organisation gets an NPWD code, operator name, company registration number and registered office. At this point it is nothing more than a record — it does not appear in any compliance-year data.

05

Created, but inert

Regulator (EA)04:45

The bottom of the same record makes the state explicit: no users, no applications for approval, “This scheme is not approved.”

06

Add the first user

Regulator (EA)05:10

The regulator creates the scheme’s first user: username, contact details, a primary role (read only / standard / super user) and the additional roles of authorised signatory and delegated authority — so authority information is captured before the scheme ever signs in. Before granting those extra permissions, the EA asks for more information about the users and managers and vets the company — Companies House checks and as much wider research as it can do — to confirm things are as they are being presented.

Observation

There is essentially no validation on this form itself; the assurance comes from the off-system vetting done before the roles are granted.

07

The email log

Regulator (EA)06:15

On the stage system all outbound mail is blocked and listed here; in live, the user gets the message directly. The same screen doubles as a log of every notification NPWD has sent.

Observation

Gareth flagged this as genuinely useful — being able to prove someone was (or wasn’t) notified. Worth keeping in the new service.

08

The invitation email

Compliance scheme06:35

The new user receives an email with an active link to set their password and PIN — they do not have to find their own way back to the service.

09

Set a PIN

Compliance scheme07:05

Alongside the password, every user sets a six-digit PIN, which NPWD then demands as a signature on nearly every significant action.

Observation

Re-entering the PIN at every step is a recognised pain point. A pen test recommended adding multi-factor authentication at every step on top — unaffordable to build in NPWD, and painful if it had been.

Phase 3 · steps 10–16

The scheme applies

The scheme logs in for the first time and works through a ten-step application for approval — started early in the compliance year because it applies to the following one. Most of the form is generic organisation detail; the batteries-specific substance is the scheme rules, conviction declarations and the operational plan.

10

Terms and conditions

Compliance scheme07:45

First login lands on a very long terms page, © 2010. “I Agree” sits at the very bottom.

Observation

A real user recently got stuck here because they couldn’t find the I Agree button.

11

The pre-approval dashboard

Compliance scheme08:05

The welcome page shows the application for approval as “Not Started”, and everything else — member templates, quarterly returns, evidence — locked until approval. Starting the application is the only thing the scheme can do.

12

Start the application

Compliance scheme08:35

The scheme picks the year it is applying for — here 2027, the following compliance year. The on-screen guidance sets the statutory window: apply between 15 April and 15 May for approval effective the next compliance period.

13

Ten steps of organisation detail

Compliance scheme10:50

Steps 1–6 collect operator, contact and address details. Little conveniences matter at this scale: “To copy the Contact Address click here” saves re-keying the same address across steps — a pattern Gareth would like kept.

14

Step 7 — proposed scheme rules

Compliance scheme12:15

The regulatory heart of the application. The scheme confirms its rules provide for: minimum one-period membership (cancellation taking effect at period end); an arbitration procedure for disputes; accurate and timely dissemination of information to members; and allocation of battery evidence notes in proportion to each member’s share of batteries placed on the market. A copy of the scheme rules must be uploaded.

Observation

These four confirmations map directly to the Waste Batteries and Accumulators Regulations 2009 — the part of the journey that fed straight into the data model. Upload validation is presence-only: any document passes.

15

Step 8 — conviction declarations

Compliance scheme13:20

Yes/no declarations of convictions under Regulation 89(2) of the batteries regulations, Regulation 73 of the WEEE regulations and Regulation 40 of the packaging regulations, each with an optional evidence upload.

Observation

In practice a tick-box for the regs: if a scheme answered dishonestly, the EA would only find out through its own investigatory work. Step 9, the operational plan upload, follows — shown later in the regulator’s review.

16

Submitted

Compliance scheme15:45

With all ten steps complete the scheme submits, signing with PIN characters. NPWD confirms the application for approval has been submitted online.

Phase 4 · steps 17–24

Regulator reviews and approves

The application now moves through Started → Submitted → Accepted → Approved on the regulator’s side, with a per-step query mechanism, a hard-coded fee, and a manually typed approval number along the way.

17

The regulator’s dashboard

Regulator (EA)16:20

Batteries Home shows submissions by type for the selected year — applications for approval, member detail submissions, quarterly returns. The regulator switches the year to 2027 to find the new application.

Observation

There is no notification when an application is submitted; the dashboard is the only signal. The two sides are usually in regular contact anyway, but an email alert would be a welcome improvement, given how rarely new schemes appear.

18

Applications by stage

Regulator (EA)16:45

Applications are listed by stage — including ones started but never finished, which Gareth finds handy. MPG’s application sits in the Submitted list with its dates.

19

Review each step — and query it

Regulator (EA)17:50

The regulator opens the application and works through all ten steps, here step 9’s uploaded operational plan. To query one part, they untick that step’s acknowledgement, “Return to Steps”, and return the application — a per-step query rather than rejecting the whole application, which sends the scheme a notification.

Observation

The operational plan is the substance of the review — is the scheme viable with the members it has? The EA offers templates but the regulations don’t mandate them, so plans arrive in every shape. Plans are only legally required at approval, though the EA asks yearly. Gareth will arrange an example plan to share. Keeping the per-step query in the new service matters.

20

Accept — enter the fee

Regulator (EA)18:45

Accepting the application requires recording the application fee in a “For Agency Use Only” panel: amount, received date, payment method. Here £1,000 is deliberately entered to test the validation.

21

“The correct fee has not been received”

Regulator (EA)19:00

NPWD rejects anything other than £17,000 — the fee is hard-coded, as all NPWD fees are. No payment actually moves through the system; this is just a record. Invoicing happens separately in SOP, where matching a payment back to submissions can take real detective work — one operator can pay for packaging and batteries in a single payment.

Observation

Make fees configurable in the new service. The £17k fee is itself a barrier to entry — if it ever changes, the trickle of new schemes could become a queue.

22

Accepted

Regulator (EA)20:35

With the correct fee recorded, the application moves to the Accepted stage. Internally this is where sign-off above the account manager (the STO) happens, but functionally little separates accepted from approved — it is an extra fail-safe in the process.

23

Type the approval number

Regulator (EA)22:05

Approval requires the regulator to enter an approval number by hand — “BCS…” — in a format set by the operational instruction “Determination of applications for approval of schemes under the Waste Batteries and Accumulators Regulations 2009”. There is no generator and no format validation.

Observation

Gareth couldn’t find the number format in the regulations themselves and is checking the operational instruction.

24

Approved

Regulator (EA)22:35

The application status becomes Approved with the approval number recorded (here BCS812394). The scheme is now in operation for the following compliance year.

Phase 5 · steps 25

After approval

Approval transforms what the scheme can do — the dashboard unlocks, and the annual rhythm of member confirmations and data returns begins.

25

The unlocked dashboard

Compliance scheme24:25

The scheme’s welcome page now shows the approval, an evidence and obligation summary for the approved year, member detail submissions and the four quarterly data returns. The first member upload is all joiners; from then on, every submission confirms joiners and leavers against the member list — the “registration” spreadsheet in the team’s notes (v2013, provenance unknown) belongs to this annual confirmation, not to account creation.

What the walkthrough says about the new service

New schemes are rare — a handful exist, all regulated by the EA in England, and the £17,000 fee keeps the queue short — so this journey is not an early build priority. It was captured for the data model and the service blueprint. The improvements Gareth raised along the way:

Notify on submission

An email to the regulator when an application (or any stage change) lands, instead of dashboard-watching.

Configurable fees

All NPWD fees are hard-coded; the new service should hold them as data.

Guidance where the work happens

A place in the service to hold or link best-practice operational-plan guidance — a prompt, not a mandated wizard; the regs let schemes submit plans in any form, and a full wizard would be too heavy.

Assisted document review

Tooling that scans an uploaded operational plan against the review questions. The EA is already exploring Copilot for this; in-service would be better.

Keep the per-step query

Returning individual steps, and seeing every stage an application has been through, is valued — preserve it.

Keep the communications log

A record of every notification sent, to prove who was told what and when.

Rethink the PIN

Signing every action with a six-digit PIN adds friction without modern assurance — Defra ID sign-in replaces the model.

Fit with the shared account model

Seen end to end, the journey is mostly generic: create an organisation, create a user, apply, review, approve. Only the scheme rules, conviction declarations and operational plan are batteries-specific — the rest overlays almost exactly onto the Manage Account team’s EPR account structure. One caution from the EA: a name shared across waste streams is not a shared team. Valpack-the-packaging-scheme and Valpack-the-batteries-scheme are entirely different groups of people, so each waste stream needs its own scheme instance beneath any parent organisation — with a parent-level view for those who need the whole picture.
